COUNCIL: World Health Organization

What WHO is:
The World Health Organization (WHO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations that focuses on international public health. It works with countries around the world to improve health and prevent diseases.

Its purpose and mandate:
The purpose of the WHO is to improve health and healthcare around the world. The WHO works on different health issues, including diseases, healthcare access, medical research and the health of different groups of people. It also helps countries create better healthcare systems and respond better to health issues.

Powers & limitations:
The WHO does not have the power to create laws that countries must follow or directly control the healthcare systems of countries. Instead, it creates recommendations, guidelines and health standards that countries can choose to follow. The WHO also works with governments and other international organizations to improve healthcare.

Types of issues the WHO addresses:
The WHO mainly looks at problems that affect people's health around the world. For example, it can look at diseases, healthcare access, medical treatment, and health problems affecting different groups of people. It can also look at how healthcare systems can be improved and how countries can work together to deal with health problems.

Why this council is relevant to the topics being discussed:
The WHO is relevant to these topics because it focuses on improving healthcare for people around the world. Adolescents and elderly people can have different physical health needs that require different types of care. At the same time, gender bias in medical research can affect how diseases and treatments are studied and understood.

TOPIC 1: Targeting Care for Adolescent & Elderly Physical Health

Adolescents and elderly people can have different physical health needs because their bodies are at different stages of development. Adolescents are still growing and going through major physical changes, while elderly people can experience changes in their bodies as they age. This means that the same treatment or healthcare approach may not always work in the same way for every age group.

Medical research has often focused on adults, which can create gaps in information about how certain treatments affect adolescents and elderly people. This can make it harder for doctors to know which treatments, medicines, and doses are most suitable for these age groups.

Adolescents can experience health problems connected to growth, nutrition, injuries and other physical changes. Elderly people are more likely to experience conditions such as limited movement, falls and multiple health conditions at the same time.

Clinical trials and medical research also face difficulties when studying these groups. Researchers have to often consider safety, age, physical development and other differences when testing treatments.

The issue remains important because better research into age specific health can help healthcare workers understand how treatments affect different age groups and provide more suitable care.

Key Issues & Challenges

Age-Specific Research:
Adolescent and elderly bodies can react differently to diseases, medicines and treatments. However, there is not always enough research that focuses specifically on these age groups.

Clinical Trials:
Including adolescents and elderly people in clinical trials can be difficult because of safety concerns and differences in their physical health. This can make it harder to collect enough information about treatments.

Medicine and Treatment:
The same medicine can affect people differently depending on their age and physical condition. Finding the correct treatment and dosage can therefore be difficult.

Lack of Data:
There are gaps in medical information about some health conditions in adolescents and elderly people. This can make it harder for doctors and researchers to make decisions based on enough evidence.

International Perspectives

Countries have different approaches to medical research involving adolescents and elderly people. Some countries have developed specific guidelines for researching medicines and treatments for these age groups, while others have fewer resources for age specific research.

Countries with larger elderly populations may tend to focus more on researching age related diseases and long-term health. Other countries may focus more on adolescent health because of their younger populations.

Previous International Action

The World Health Organization has worked on improving healthcare for adolescents and elderly people by supporting approaches that consider health throughout a person's life.

The WHO has also developed guidance for adolescent health and the Integrated Care for Older People (ICOPE) approach, which focuses on providing suitable and coordinated care for elderly people.

TOPIC 2: Addressing Gender Bias in Medical Research and Clinical Trials

Gender bias in medical research happens when medical research does not properly consider differences between men and women. This can affect how diseases are studied, how treatments are tested and how medical results are used.

In the past, some medical research focused more on men than women. This created gaps in information about how certain diseases and treatments could affect women differently.

The issue became more widely discussed as researchers found that men were often more heavily represented in medical research and clinical trials. For years, some studies focused mainly on the bodies of males, while women were included less often. This created gaps in medical knowledge about how certain diseases and treatments affect women specifically.

This can be a problem because men and women can experience some diseases differently and may react differently to certain medicines and treatments. When research is mainly based on men, the results may not fully represent how a treatment will affect women.

The issue remains important because medical research is used to develop medicines and treatments for everyone. If women are not properly represented in research, it can lead to gaps in healthcare and make some treatments less suitable for women.

Key Issues & Challenges

Representation in Research:
Women have been underrepresented in some areas of medical research. This can create gaps in information about how diseases and treatments affect women.

Clinical Trials:
Including different groups in clinical trials can be difficult because of recruitment, health risks and other factors. Researchers must also make sure that trials remain safe for participants.

Research Methods:
Differences between men and women can affect how diseases develop and how treatments work. Researchers may disagree about how these differences should be included when designing and studying research.

International Perspectives

Countries have taken different approaches to gender bias in medical research. Some countries have introduced policies to encourage or require greater inclusion of women in clinical trials, while others have fewer specific requirements.

The United States has introduced policies requiring women to be included in certain federally funded clinical research. It has also encouraged researchers to consider gender when conducting medical research.

Other countries and international organizations have also focused on improving representation in medical research. However, countries have different research systems, laws and resources, which can affect how these policies are used.

Previous International Action

The United States introduced policies requiring women to be included in federally funded clinical research. The National Institutes of Health also introduced policies requiring researchers to consider gender as a biological factor in certain areas of research.
