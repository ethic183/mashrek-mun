✦ Council Overview

The International Court of Justice (ICJ) is the principal judicial organ of the United Nations. It settles legal disputes between States and provides advisory opinions on questions of international law. The Court bases its decisions on international treaties, customary international law, and other recognized sources of international law. Judges must examine the facts of a case, determine whether the Court has jurisdiction, interpret the relevant law, and reach an impartial judgment.

Unlike political MUN committees, the ICJ does not negotiate resolutions. Judges must focus on the legal questions presented by the parties and determine whether international law has been breached.

✦ Topic A

Russian Federation v. Canada — Antonov AN-124

1. Introduction & Background

Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Canada closed its airspace to Russian aircraft. A Russian Antonov AN-124 aircraft operated by Volga-Dnepr Airlines was at Toronto Pearson International Airport when the restrictions were introduced and was subsequently prevented from leaving Canada.

Canada later took steps to seize the aircraft under its sanctions legislation. In June 2023, the Canadian Government issued an order providing for the seizure of the aircraft under the Special Economic Measures Act. Canada stated that the aircraft was connected to Volga-Dnepr Airlines and was subject to Canada's sanctions framework.

The dispute raises questions about the relationship between international aviation law, State sanctions, property rights, and the obligations of States during international disputes. The Court must consider the specific legal obligations applicable to the parties rather than the broader political disagreement surrounding the war in Ukraine.

2. Key Legal Issues & Challenges

A Jurisdiction

The Court must first determine whether it has jurisdiction over the dispute. The ICJ can only hear a contentious case where the States involved have consented to its jurisdiction through an applicable legal basis.

The judges must therefore consider whether an applicable treaty or other legal instrument gives the Court jurisdiction and whether the dispute falls within that instrument.

B International Aviation Law

The detention of the aircraft raises questions concerning the rights and obligations of States under international aviation agreements.

The Court may need to consider the extent to which Canada could restrict the aircraft's ability to operate or leave its territory and whether such restrictions were consistent with Canada's international obligations.

C Seizure of the Aircraft

The later seizure of the aircraft creates a separate legal question from its initial detention. Judges must consider what international rules apply to the seizure of property connected to a foreign State or foreign company and whether Canada's domestic sanctions legislation is consistent with its international obligations.

D State Responsibility and Remedies

If the Court determines that Canada breached an international obligation, it must consider whether Canada is internationally responsible and what consequences may follow.

Potential issues include restitution, compensation, or other remedies recognized under international law.

3. Positions of the Parties

Russian Federation: The Russian position concerns Canada's treatment of the aircraft and whether Canada's restrictions and subsequent seizure complied with its international obligations.

Canada: Canada has connected its actions to its response to Russia's invasion of Ukraine and its sanctions regime. Canada maintains that the aircraft was subject to measures adopted under Canadian law.

The Court must distinguish between the political context of the dispute and the specific legal claims presented before it.

4. Previous International Action

Canada closed its airspace to Russian aircraft in 2022, resulting in the continued detention of the AN-124. The International Civil Aviation Organization documented the situation.

Canada formally ordered the seizure of the aircraft in 2023 under its sanctions legislation. The aircraft has also been involved in separate legal proceedings concerning Canada's measures.

These actions provide the factual background for the dispute, but the Court must determine the legal consequences under the applicable international law.

✦ Topic B

Mauritius v. United Kingdom — Chagos Archipelago

1. Introduction & Background

The dispute concerning the Chagos Archipelago concerns the separation of the territory from Mauritius during the period of British colonial rule. In 1965, the United Kingdom separated the Chagos Archipelago from Mauritius and established the British Indian Ocean Territory. Mauritius subsequently gained independence in 1968.

The status of the Chagos Archipelago became an international legal dispute concerning decolonization, self-determination, and the territorial integrity of non-self-governing territories. The issue was brought before the ICJ through a request from the United Nations General Assembly for an advisory opinion on the legal consequences of the separation.

In its Advisory Opinion of 25 February 2019, the Court concluded that the decolonization of Mauritius had not been lawfully completed when Mauritius became independent and that the United Kingdom was under an obligation to end its administration of the Chagos Archipelago as rapidly as possible.

The case raises questions concerning the right to self-determination, territorial integrity, decolonization, and the legal consequences of the continued administration of a territory by a former colonial power.

2. Key Legal Issues & Challenges

A Jurisdiction

The Court first had to determine whether it had jurisdiction to provide the requested advisory opinion. Unlike a normal contentious case between two States, the proceedings were initiated through a request from the United Nations General Assembly under Article 96 of the UN Charter and Article 65 of the ICJ Statute. The judges therefore had to consider whether the requirements for providing an advisory opinion had been met.

B Right to Self-Determination

A central legal question concerns the right of peoples to self-determination during the process of decolonization.

The Court had to consider the international law applicable in 1965 and whether the separation of the Chagos Archipelago from Mauritius was consistent with the right of the people of a non-self-governing territory to determine their political status.

C Territorial Integrity and Decolonization

The separation of the Chagos Archipelago raises questions concerning the territorial integrity of Mauritius during the decolonization process.

The Court considered whether the detachment of the territory could be considered lawful when Mauritius was still a non-self-governing territory and whether the process of decolonization could be regarded as complete after the separation.

D Legal Consequences

The Court also had to determine the legal consequences arising from the United Kingdom's continued administration of the Chagos Archipelago.

This included considering the obligations of the United Kingdom and the role of other UN Member States in co-operating with the United Nations to complete the decolonization of Mauritius.

3. Positions of the Parties

Mauritius: Mauritius maintains that the separation of the Chagos Archipelago prevented the completion of its decolonization and has sought recognition of its position concerning the territory and the consequences of the UK's continued administration.

United Kingdom: The United Kingdom presented arguments concerning the legal circumstances surrounding the separation and its administration of the Chagos Archipelago. The UK's written statement was submitted to the ICJ during the advisory proceedings.

The Court's proceedings focused on the legal questions submitted by the General Assembly rather than simply determining the political positions of Mauritius and the United Kingdom.

4. Previous International Action

The United Nations General Assembly adopted Resolution 71/292 in 2017, requesting an advisory opinion from the ICJ concerning the legal consequences of the separation of the Chagos Archipelago from Mauritius.

The ICJ issued its Advisory Opinion on 25 February 2019, finding that the decolonization of Mauritius had not been lawfully completed and that the United Kingdom was under an obligation to end its administration of the Chagos Archipelago as rapidly as possible. The Court also stated that all Member States were under an obligation to cooperate with the United Nations to complete the decolonization of Mauritius.

The Advisory Opinion provides the main legal framework for the issue. The remaining questions concern the implementation of the Court's conclusions, the future status of the Archipelago, and the rights and interests of the Chagossian population.
